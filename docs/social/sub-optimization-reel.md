## Transcript (1:31)

Can you coach a client to do a movement exactly right, but in the process make
the whole session worse? In engineering, this is called sub-optimization.
Picture a race team that pours everything into the car's engine. They tune it
so precisely. They've got everything dialed in. It is the most powerful engine
on the circuit. But then they mount it in a frame that wasn't also designed to
handle that amount of power. Or the tires weren't designed to transfer that
power from the engine through the frame to the asphalt. They wear out in half
the laps. It's a perfect engine, but it's actually a slower car.

I see this all the time in Pilates. We get so focused on micro-movements and
precision that oftentimes we lose sight of what we want the client to leave
with at the end of the hour, or even at the end of six weeks. Take footwork for
example. We can obsess about the proper foot, knee, hip, even pelvis alignment
during footwork. It can take up 20 minutes of a session. But the client might
be holding their breath, or the springs are too light to really challenge them.
And you've lost time to get in a good workout for the rest of the body. Perfect
alignment in footwork, but worse overall session.

Don't get me wrong, precision has its place. I'm not saying we shouldn't be
precise in Pilates. But it has to fit into the bigger picture of what we're
trying to achieve with our clients.
