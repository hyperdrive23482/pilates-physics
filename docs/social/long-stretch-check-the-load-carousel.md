## Slides

1. You think it's a weak core. I'd check the load first.
2. I taught long stretch prep last night in my level 1 reformer class. Before we moved the arms, we checked the load and made individual adjustments.
3. Some people added or subtracted a spring. Others walked their feet forward or backward on the carriage.
4. Why? Because the same setting loads every client differently.
5. Here are 3 examples of me doing long stretch on different spring settings.
6. Notice 2 things in each: 1. How close can I start to the footbar? 2. How far out can I press the carriage?
7. [video] Balanced Body red + blue springs. 14"-25" extension (36-64 cm). 37-56 lbs range (17-25kg).
8. [video] Balanced Body red + yellow springs. 12"-30" extension (30-76 cm). 28-52 lbs range (13-24kg).
9. [video] Balanced Body red spring. 11"-17" extension (28-43 cm). 19-25 lbs range (8.5-11kg).
10. Red alone lets me start over the footbar, but I run out of range. Adding spring weight costs me that start and buys range, until it's too heavy and I lose range again.
11. Now, what if I were 5'4" (163 cm) tall instead of 5'9" (175 cm)?
12. Starting Spring Weights table. Me / Short Me. Red + blue: 14" 37 lbs / 9" 29 lbs. Red + yellow: 12" 28 lbs / 7" 21 lbs. Red: 11" 19 lbs / 6" 14 lbs.
13. As you can see, different people on the same springs feel different loads.
14. It's ridiculous to think that there is one ideal spring weight everyone should strive for.
15. Before you call it a weak core, poor form, or lack of coordination, check the load.

## Caption

Before deciding your client just needs to get stronger, check the load.

I taught long stretch prep in my level 1 reformer class last night. Before anyone moved their arms, I had everyone check the load in a plank position. Some people added or subtracted a spring. Others walked their feet forward or backward on the carriage.

Here's why. Springs get heavier the further they stretch. In long stretch, your hands are on the footbar and your feet are on the carriage, so the length of your body helps set how far the springs are stretched before you press.

So when long stretch falls apart, ask this first: would adjusting the load from the springs help support the client more?  Challenge the client more?
