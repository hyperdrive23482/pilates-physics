# Carousel: Do the hamstrings pull the carriage in?

## Slide 1 (cover)

**Your hamstrings *don't* pull the carriage in.**

Not in footwork. Here's the physics.

## Slide 2

"Use your hamstrings to pull the carriage home." It sounds logical, since the knee bends on the return and hamstrings bend the knee. But follow the energy and the story changes.

## Slide 3

Energy is never created or destroyed, it only changes hands. On the reformer, your muscles, the springs and the carriage wheels trade it back and forth all rep long.

## Slide 4

When a muscle shortens under load (concentric), it gives energy to the system. When it lengthens under load (eccentric), it absorbs energy.

## Slide 5

On the push out, your quads and glutes contract concentrically. Energy flows from your muscles into the springs and wheels.

## Slide 6

On the return, the springs give that energy back and your body absorbs it. Absorbing is eccentric work, done by the same quads and glutes that pushed out.

## Slide 7

Push out or return, the quads and glutes do the work. The hamstrings never take the lead in either direction.

## Slide 8

Squeeze your hamstrings on the return and you add energy the springs are already supplying. Your quads and glutes then work harder to absorb it. That's co-contraction, not a new prime mover.

## Slide 9

The only way the hamstrings lead is to out-pull the springs, drawing the carriage home faster than the springs would alone. Pressing down into the footbar adds hamstring too, but that's drifting into a bridge.

## Caption

"Pull the carriage in with your hamstrings." It's one of the most common footwork cues out there, and it feels logical. The knee bends on the way in, and hamstrings bend the knee.

But follow the energy. On the push out, your muscles give energy to the springs. On the return, the springs give it back, and your body absorbs it. Absorbing energy is eccentric work, and the muscles doing it are your quads and glutes, the same ones that pushed out.

Squeeze the hamstrings on the return and you can feel them, sure. But you've added a co-contraction, not a new prime mover. Your quads and glutes still do the job, plus a little extra.
