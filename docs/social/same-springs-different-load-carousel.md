1. Every cue I used failed. / Then I added a spring.
2. I was teaching a group class bridge on the reformer. / My intent was to focus on articulation: rolling up one spinal segment at a time.
3. For one of my clients, the only way up for them was to *grip*. / Everything above the pelvis moved as a block. / But they were on the same springs as everyone else.
4. *“Rock your pelvis.”* / *“Peel your back up one bone at a time.”* / *“Move your spine like a wave.”* / Nothing landed.
5. So I added a spring. / More support. / The articulation showed up almost immediately.
6. More spring made it easier to achieve the intent. / That shouldn't be possible if the same setting were the same exercise for everyone.
7. Here's why. / The external load at a joint comes from two places: / the *springs*, and the *body itself*.
8. Since joints rotate, external loads apply *torque* to a joint. / And the exact torque is different for everyone because our bodies are all unique sizes.
9. Torque = force × distance / How much force the load applies, in what direction, a perpendicular distance from the joint.
10. Let's apply some realistic numbers to help explain the impact different variables have on the load someone feels. / (You don't need to do math *while* teaching, though.)
11. Teaser on the Mat / 5'5" (165 cm) person / 130 lbs / 227 lb-in / 250 lbs / 436 lb-in / 92% more load at the hip.
12. Feet in Straps / 5'9" (175 cm) person / red + blue BB springs / 150 lbs / +89 lb-in / 300 lbs / -260 lb-in / Positive: springs win. / Press the legs *down*. / Negative: legs win. / Hold the legs *up*.
13. Same springs.  Same movement. / *Opposite jobs* for  the muscles.
14. That's what happened in my class during bridge. / The room's setting was more load than one of my students' could find the movement under.
15. Before you change the cue *again*, check the load. / Know your intent. / Ask what they feel. / Then adjust the settings that matter.
16. The same spring setting / is not the same workout for everyone.

## Caption

Every cue I used failed. Adding a spring is what finally worked.

A client in my bridge class was on the same springs as the rest of the room, and the only way up was to grip. More spring gave them the support to articulate.

The external load at a joint comes from the springs and from the body itself. Change the body and you change the load, even when the springs stay the same. I ran the numbers for Teaser and Feet in Straps in my latest blog post.
