## Slides

1. How does the load during Feet in Straps compare between clients of different sizes?
2. Here's what I mean when I say the same spring settings aren't the same for different people.
3. Look at hip joint torque during Feet in Straps: how much is the net torque at the hip joint from the weight of the legs and the springs combined?
4. Essentially, how much external load does the person have to be able to control?
5. [diagram] Rope angle, spring force, leg angle, leg center of mass, leg weight.
6. Here are my assumptions: single leg weight 18% of body weight; leg length 49% of height; leg COM 43% of distance from hip to ankle; springs BB red + blue; leg angle 30 deg above horizontal; rope angle 6 deg below horizontal.
7. Torque is created from a force applied a certain distance from the joint. τ = F · d
8. Change the distance or the force, and the torque changes.
9. [diagram] Distances to forces.
10. Let's compare a 5'9" person who weighs 150 lbs, and one who weighs 300 lbs.
11. 5'9" (175 cm) tall person. 150 lbs (68 kg): 89 lb-in (10 Nm). 300 lbs (136 kg): -260 lb-in (-29 Nm).
12. Same, circled: "But wait, why is one negative but not the other?"
13. The one that's positive means that person has to work to press their legs down. The springs are exerting more torque around the hip than the weight of the legs.
14. The one that's negative means the person has to work to hold their legs up. The weight of the legs is exerting more torque around the hip than the springs.
15. Don't worry about doing the math for every client. They can tell you what they feel.
16. Use this as an example of how two people can be on the same spring settings yet feel a VERY different type and amount of load.
17. This difference in experience is exactly why it's important to know your intent... Do you want the client to have to work to press down? Or pull up?
18. And the role the springs, reformer components, and adjustments play in supporting or resisting that movement.
19. Because then you will feel confident knowing how to adjust the equipment for a client who isn't feeling what you were hoping for.

## Caption

Here's an example of how the same springs create a different experience for different people.

I did some math for feet in straps, two clients that are the same height but different weights.  One had to work to press their legs down, and one had to work to hold their legs up.

You don't have to do math when you teach, but you do need to know what your intention is and how to fix the settings if it's not being achieved.
