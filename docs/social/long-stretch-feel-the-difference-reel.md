## Transcript (2:00)

One of my favorite exercises to really feel how the same settings are totally
different feelings for different sized people is long stretch. So if you want
to follow along with me at home, grab a full spring and a quarter spring and
let's hop on and put your feet in the middle of the carriage. Find your plank
position. Notice where the challenge is. How close are your shoulders to the
foot bar? And then take a few press outs.

Good. So this is maybe your height. Now, if we were to do this same exercise,
but say you were six inches shorter, step backwards six inches on the
carriage. Find your starting position and press out and in. Notice, can you
press out as far? Is there more or less work in your shoulders or your
abdominals?

Now, if you come back to the middle of the carriage and you want to feel how
this exercise is different for someone who's six inches taller than you, step
forward six inches and press out. See, I can't even press out, my feet are
sliding, the springs are too heavy. In order to do that I have to take a
spring off to feel the same thing as I did when my feet were in the middle of
the carriage.

Okay, so that is the perfect example of how the same spring settings feel
different to different people. And sometimes that difference is okay, but a
lot of times it means that your clients aren't feeling what you feel in your
own body. And so knowing, okay, here's the memorized starting point of our
settings, and then being able to reason through how to adapt the exercise for
someone who is a different strength or a different size than the model in the
training manual is critical for making your intention land.

## Caption (scheduled)

Your spring settings were written for someone else's body. Here's how to feel it.

Long stretch on a full spring and a quarter spring. First with my feet in the middle of the carriage, my usual spot. Then I step back six inches to feel what a client six inches shorter feels. Then forward six inches for a client six inches taller.

Stepping back, I can't press out as far, and the work shifts in my shoulders and abs.

Stepping forward, my feet slide. The springs win. I had to take a spring off just to feel what I felt in the middle.

The springs don't know how tall you are. But your height changes where your feet land relative to the footbar, and that changes your angle in the plank. The angle decides how much of your body weight helps you hold the carriage against the springs, and how much lands on your shoulders and abs.

So when a client says an exercise feels nothing like it should, ask yourself: is the setting wrong, or was it written for a different body? The manual gives you a starting point. Reasoning from it is how your intention lands.

Try it next time you're on a reformer and tell me which version surprised you.
