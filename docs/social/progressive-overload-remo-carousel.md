## Caption

Progressive overload is the term that decides whether something gets taken
seriously as strength work. Emphasis on progressive.

We talk about progress plenty. It usually sounds like "you look stronger," or a
client finally nailing teaser, or someone telling us they feel better. All of
those are real. But what did they do last session, and what makes today harder?

Most of the time we answer that in the moment, from how they move that day and
what we're excited to teach next. It's a real skill. It just leaves no trail.

Strength training has load, sets and reps, easy to write down and easy to track.
In Pilates, springs change, modifications evolve, layers get added. Every one of
those is progress, and recording it is not as simple as writing numbers in a
table. So the progress is not missing, it is unwritten. And unwritten progress is
the kind your client never gets to see.

Most veteran teachers have a trail of abandoned notetaking methods behind them,
and they all get abandoned for the same reason. Not discipline. There is no time
or energy left after a day of teaching.

We notice improvements in our clients. We are just not built to store them long
term and share them on demand. That is why I built Remo.

## Changes from the Canva build

1. **Slide 18 split into 18 and 19.** The Canva slide runs seven lines and
   carries the emotional payoff. "No late-night report writing required" is the
   punchline and lands harder alone. This takes the deck to 20, still within
   Instagram's limit.
2. **Slide 8: "represents real progress" to "is progress."** "Real" appears on
   slides 4, 5 and 8 in the Canva build, and it dulls on a swipe. Slide 4 already
   established these are real, so 8 does not need to re-argue it.
3. **Slide 5: "excited to teach" to "excited to teach next."** The honesty of
   that line is why it works, but without "next" a skeptic reads it as confirming
   that Pilates is unsystematic. "Next" implies a plan rather than a whim and
   costs one word.
4. **Cover emoji dropped** from this file. Keep it in Canva if you want it; the
   thread-spool emoji reads as a Twitter-thread convention, which is a different
   platform's grammar.

## What this draft got right, worth preserving through future edits

- **Slide 2.** Keeping "progressive overload" and redirecting the emphasis to
  "progressive" resolves the overload-versus-loading tension without conceding
  the frame. Do not trade this back.
- **Slides 3 to 5.** These replaced an assertion ("nobody has agreed how to
  measure it") with a demonstration. Slide 4's question is the sharpest thing in
  the deck.
- **"It's a real skill, it just leaves no trail."** Honors the reader and names
  the gap in eight words, and rhymes with the trail of abandoned notetaking
  methods on slide 11. Keep both, and keep them in that order.
- **Slides 16 to 18.** Three beats that answer, in order, the no-time slide, the
  no-trail slide, and the client-never-sees slide. The payoff now maps onto the
  setup.
