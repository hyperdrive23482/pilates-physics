## The job

Handle the one objection this product actually has, which per the course spec is
**"I already know my machine."** Not "I hate math." These people opted into a
spring calculator, so they have already self-selected as the technical segment.

The self-quiz does it without arguing with anyone. Five questions about equipment
they touch every day, answered privately, scored by them. Nobody has to be told
they are wrong. They find out on their own, which is the only version of this
that does not make an experienced teacher defensive.

It is also close to unopenable-proof. A subject line offering five questions
about your own reformer is hard to leave sitting there.

## Design rules

- **Ask, do not answer.** The email reveals no answers, and says so explicitly.
  That is not a tease, because the product is named, priced, and one click away
  in the same email. A quiz that answered itself would have nothing to sell and
  would also be a worse quiz.
- **No gotchas.** Every question has to be one a good, experienced teacher could
  reasonably not know, and none of them should feel like trivia. If a question
  makes the reader feel stupid rather than curious, it is the wrong question.
- **Give permission not to buy.** "If you got all five, you do not need this" is
  what makes the rest of it credible. Say it and mean it.
- **Each question maps to a module,** so the quiz doubles as a syllabus nobody
  has to read.

## The five questions and what they map to

| # | Question | Module | The trap |
|---|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Gear bar out one notch. What happens to the load, and what else moves with it | 3, adjustments | Most teachers know it gets lighter. Almost nobody has been taught the second-order effects, which is the whole trickle-down hook |
| 2 | Same color spring, two different brands. Same resistance | 2, springs | Color is a brand convention, not a unit. This one lands hardest on anyone who has taught in more than one studio |
| 3 | The rope runs through a pulley, so your client feels half the spring load | 4, pulleys | "Half is half true." The spec's A/B ad hook, and the thing teachers say out loud to clients |
| 4 | A heavier client makes the carriage harder to push, because of friction | 5, friction | The myth-bust, and the one most likely to change how somebody thinks |
| 5 | Your oldest spring. How would you know if it still does what its color says | 2, wear | The honest caveat the free calculator leaves open, and the question studio owners feel in their wallet |

**Coverage check:** modules 2, 3, 4, and 5. That is the paid core of the course.
Modules 1, 6, and 7 are anatomy, classical vs contemporary, and the handoff,
none of which produce a good quiz question.

**Question 1 is deliberately not phrased with a count.** The spec's open decision
on how many real second-order effects live in module 3 is unresolved, so the
question asks "what else moves with it" rather than promising three things. If
the count gets confirmed, this line can get sharper.

Hi {{ subscriber.first_name }},

Do you know your reformer as well as you think you do?

You may have taught on the same reformer for years and know exactly what it does. You've experimented with different settings and know through trial and error that moving one thing affects another thing.

Regardless, here's a little quiz to help you think about what you do and don't know about your reformer.

**Three.** The rope runs through a pulley, so your client feels half the spring load. True or false?

**Four.** A heavier client makes the carriage significantly harder to push, because of friction. True or false?

**Five.** Why do manufacturers recommend replacing your springs so often?

## Notes

- **Neither version answers a single question,** which is deliberate and stated
  in the copy. The honesty of saying "I am not going to answer them here, that is
  the course" is what keeps this from reading as a tease.
- **Both give explicit permission not to buy.** Do not cut that line to save
  space. It is what makes an experienced teacher willing to take the test.
- **The Kit score segmentation from the original spec is dropped.** In the old
  four-email sales sequence the quiz ran on day 2 so day 3 could hit low scorers
  harder. In this structure email 6 is the last content email and email 7 is a
  reminder, so there is nothing to segment into. That also means no interactive
  build is needed and this can ship as plain text.
- **Version B's opening claim needs a fact check from Kaleen.** "I could not have
  answered these" and "I missed three of them" in subject line 3 have to be
  actually true. If they are not, use version A. This is the same capture problem
  as email 5 and is worth resolving with story bank section 4.
- **Question 4's P.S. is the strongest line in either version** and is the spec's
  "friction is not the problem you think it is" hook. Keep it wherever you land.
- **If the trickle-down count gets confirmed,** question 1 can become "what
  happens to the load, and what are the three other things that change with it,"
  which is sharper and matches the primary ad hook.
