## The job

One job. The price goes up. Here is the link.

**Target: two or three sentences.** Not a short email, an actually short one.
The previous cart-close drafts ran 60 to 90 words, which was already short. These
run 25 to 40, because email 7 went out this morning and said everything. Anyone
opening this has been told what the course is, what is in it, and what it costs,
three times. A fourth argument on the same day reads as anxiety.

## Rules

Carried over from the old email 7 drafts, all still current:

- **No guilt, no fake scarcity.** The price genuinely goes to $69 and the course
  genuinely stays available. Say exactly that.
- **State what actually happens at the deadline.** The content is not going away.
  Only the price changes. Being precise about it is more persuasive than being
  vague, and it is the spec's urgency lever verbatim: price only, all content
  always included.
- **Link early.** People skim a closing email to the button.
- **No P.S.** Consistent with emails 4 through 7, and there is nothing to put in
  one.
- **No new argument, no new hook, no CEC.** By now any of the three is a new
  sale starting at the wrong hour.
- **Name a real time.** "Midnight" is a placeholder in all three drafts. Match it
  to whatever the cart actually enforces and name the timezone if the list spans
  more than one.

## Notes

- **Version A is the default.** Three sentences, no claim that needs checking, and
  it works whether or not the reader opened anything today.
- **Version C depends on email 7 having landed.** It is fine if it did not, since
  the sentence still parses, but the extra warmth only pays off for openers.
- **All three keep "the course stays available" or imply it.** Version B is the
  exception and it is the one thing B gives up. If the honest-close tone matters
  more than the word count, A over B.
- **This is the only evening send in the sequence.** Emails 1 through 7 all go in
  the morning. Worth confirming the list's evening open rate before assuming this
  lands, and worth checking Kit's send-time settings do not have a list-wide
  morning default that will quietly override it.
- **Nobody gets a next-time tease,** because there is no next email. If a main-list
  newsletter follows, that transition is a separate send.

## After this email

The sequence ends here. Per the funnel structure, subscribers move to the main
list. Two things are undecided and both sit outside this file:

1. **What happens to non-buyers.** They have had one offer and declined it. The
   spec puts the course back in front of them as a permanent $39 order bump at
   Pilates Physics 101 checkout, which is the natural second chance.
2. **What happens to buyers.** They should not get the main list cold. The spec
   has an open decision about where the same-day action goes, and a post-purchase
   email carrying the spring inspection checklist is one of the listed options.
