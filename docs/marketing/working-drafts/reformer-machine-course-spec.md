# The Making of a Reformer: Course and Offer Spec

> **Status: working draft.** Nothing in this file is approved. CEC approval is
> unconfirmed and the discount enforcement mechanism is deliberately deferred.

**The Making of a Reformer**
*How your machine works and why*

An on-demand mini course on the reformer as a machine. Proposed to fill the
tripwire slot in the funnel described in
[funnel-strategy-nikki-session.md](../funnel-strategy-nikki-session.md).

---

## Why this instead of the body weight mini course

Nikki's original tripwire was a body weight and dimensions mini course. The
problem is that body weight is Pilates Physics 101's thesis, not an on-ramp to
it. Selling the workshop's central claim at tripwire price either spoils the
workshop or makes it look redundant.

A machine-only primer fixes that by making the ladder about scope rather than
depth. Each rung is strictly wider than the one below it, so finishing one makes
the next feel incomplete rather than optional.

| Rung | Scope |
|------|-------|
| Spring calculator (free) | One spring, one number |
| This course (paid, low) | The whole machine. Everything that changes load before a body touches it |
| Pilates Physics 101 | What happens when a body meets that load |
| Pilates Physics 102 | The other apparatus |

The calculator sets it up. It answers "what does this spring weigh at
extension," which immediately raises "what else on this machine changes that
number." This course is the answer to a question the lead magnet already
planted.

Secondary advantages:

- **Low defensiveness.** The body weight angle implies the teacher has been
  loading clients wrong. The machine angle is about equipment, so it is an
  easier yes at a low price.
- **Buildable from existing assets.** Animations, the spring calculator, the
  class simulator. No clients on camera, no shoot days.
- **Repeatable product shape.** Reformer, then chair, then Cadillac, each one
  feeding the matching workshop.

## Relationship to Pilates Physics 101

The overlap is PP101 sections 1 and 2 only (Hooke's law and spring behavior;
reformer adjustments and pulleys). Sections 3 through 5 (force vectors, body
dimensions, joint torque) are load times body and stay untouched.

**Do not remove sections 1 and 2 from PP101.** People buy 101 cold off the sales
page without ever seeing this funnel, so it has to stand alone, and "helpful but
not required" is only true if 101 still teaches Hooke's law itself.

The overlap is a feature. PP101 section 2's takeaway is "most reformer
adjustments impact spring stretch, which impacts load." In 101 that is an
assertion made in a few minutes. This course is the full evidence for it, so
someone who takes it walks into 101 already believing the thing 101 has to
establish.

## The design story

Kaleen designed a reformer. That is the product's real moat and the fix for the
"here is technical information" flatness that a pure reference course falls into.
Every other person teaching reformer mechanics is explaining someone else's
machine.

It also converts the content into narrative without changing a single fact.
"Here is a decision I had to make, here is what each option does to the load,
here is what I chose and why" delivers identical technical material with stakes
attached.

### Run it as connective tissue, not as spine

Open each module with the design decision, deliver the technical content, close
with what was chosen and why. That preserves the modular structure a CEC and a
reference document both need, while the narrative carries the reader between
modules. A pure chronological story would fight both.

### What it upgrades, module by module

| Module | What the design story adds |
|--------|----------------------------|
| 2, friction | You specified wheels and a track. You know why friction is negligible because you had to decide how negligible to make it |
| 4, every dial | Every adjustment range is a designer's choice about where to stop. Where the gear bar stops, how many rope positions exist, how high the footbar goes |
| 7, classical vs contemporary | Goes from the riskiest module to the best one. You had to choose between the traditions, with reasons, so your neutrality is credible rather than diplomatic. You are not refereeing a fight you have no stake in, you are someone who had to pick and can explain the tradeoff |
| 8, springs over time | You sourced springs and evaluated coatings and fatigue life. The most credible seven minutes in the product, and nobody else in Pilates education can produce it |

### The machine stays unnamed

Following Nikki's Remo play: show it, do not name it, let people ask. The one
thing that makes that work rather than backfire is the technical content staying
demonstrably fair across brands, which the spring calculator already is. Do that
and the design story reads as credentials. Skip it and it reads as a pitch.

### The story is the title, so it has to actually be in there

The product is named after the design story, which means the story cannot be a
decorative frame around a reference course. If a buyer finishes and cannot
recount a single decision that was made and why, the title wrote a check the
content did not cash.

Practical test for each module: name the decision, name the options, name what
was chosen. Three sentences. If a module cannot produce them, the design story
does not belong in that module and it should run straight technical instead.

The same phrase carries beyond the product: a signature talk, an Instagram
series, and top-of-funnel organic content all sit under it.

## Course outline

Designed to roughly 60 minutes of contact time. Running slightly over is
deliberate. Running under is the risk for CEC purposes.

| # | Module | Min | Format | Job |
|---|--------|-----|--------|-----|
| 0 | Start here | 2 | Video | The promise: you own more machine than you are using. Opens the design story that runs through every module |
| 1 | The machine, named | 5 | Video + interactive diagram | Vocabulary. Which parts change load and which do not |
| 2 | Where load actually comes from | 5 | Video + text | Springs, carriage mass, friction. The friction myth-bust |
| 3 | Springs, actually | 10 | Video + spring calculator + checkpoint | Hooke's law, elastic vs plastic, slack vs pretension, colors lie |
| 4 | Every dial and what it does | 9 | Video + tables + tool | Gear bar, footbar, ropes, risers. Effect on stretch, no body |
| 5 | One change, three effects | 7 | Video + worked examples | The hook module. Second-order consequences |
| 6 | Pulleys and the load curve | 6 | Animation + text | One rope vs two, why "half" is half true, where load peaks |
| 7 | Classical vs contemporary | 4 | Side by side + text | Physics takes no side. Same exercise, different load, neither wrong |
| 8 | Springs over time | 7 | Video + downloadable checklist | Coatings, wear, inspection, hazards, when to call the manufacturer |
| 9 | What I did not teach you | 3 | Video | The body. Handoff to 101 |
| - | Final assessment | 5 | Quiz | CEC requirement |

**Total: 63 minutes.**

### Design decisions inside the outline

- **Elastic and plastic deformation sits early, with Hooke's law** (module 3),
  not with maintenance. Hooke's law is true in the elastic region. Past it the
  spring takes a permanent set and never returns those numbers again. Same
  lesson, two payoffs, and it makes Hooke's law feel real instead of academic.
- **Friction goes early as the trust module** (module 2). It is relief, not
  threat, so it does not sell, but an educator who tells you what you can stop
  worrying about is more credible on everything after. Build in the caveat that
  friction is negligible on a maintained machine. Flat-spotted wheels and a dry
  track are a different conversation.
- **Wear and maintenance closes the course** (module 8), because "now go look at
  your own machine" converts into same-day action.
- **Module 8 is also the honest caveat on the free calculator.** The
  calculator's numbers assume a spring still in its elastic range that has not
  taken a set and is not corroded under the coating. Answering "what about my
  twelve-year-old springs" is the missing half of the lead magnet.
- **Module 9 is a handoff, not a pitch.** Ending on what the course does not
  cover converts better than a hard close, and it preframes 101.

### Safety framing for module 8

Keep it descriptive, not prescriptive. "Here is what to look for and when to
call your manufacturer," never "safe" or "unsafe," and point to each
manufacturer's own replacement guidance rather than inventing an interval. This
is the one module where being wrong has physical consequences, so the wear
claims should have something citable behind them.

## Draft learning objectives

CEC applications generally want measurable verbs.

1. Identify the components of a reformer that change load and those that do not.
2. Explain how spring stretch determines load, and where Hooke's law stops applying.
3. Predict the effect of a given adjustment on starting load and load through range.
4. Describe the second-order effects of a single equipment adjustment.
5. Recognize signs of spring wear and describe an inspection process.

## CEC status

**Unconfirmed. Do not promise the CEC on a sales page until this is checked.**
On-demand self-study is often a separate approval category from live workshops
even for an already-recognized provider, and typically requires a post-test and
a completion certificate.

The CEC is what justifies the price. "One hour of video for $39" is a hard sell.
"1 CEC for $39" is instantly comparable to every other CEC on the market and
wins that comparison. It belongs above the fold and in every email.

## Title

> **The Making of a Reformer**
> *How your machine works and why*

The title is the one option no one else in Pilates education could ever use,
because it is only true of someone who designed the machine. It carries warmth
and access where a purely technical name carries only status, and it names the
design story that runs through the whole course.

The subtitle does the work the title does not. "How your machine works" plants
the product firmly in understanding rather than construction, which is what
keeps "The Making Of" from reading as build instructions. "And why" is the whole
premise: every part is a choice somebody made, and each choice changes the load.

**"Your machine" is doing quiet filtering work.** It implies the reader already
has one, so anyone looking for plans to build a reformer self-selects out, and
an instructor knows immediately that this is for them. Keep the possessive
framing in the H1 subline and the meta description.

### Naming rules

- **The title and subtitle travel together.** "The Making of a Reformer" alone
  is ambiguous between documentary and how-to-build. With the subtitle attached
  it is unambiguous. Do not ship the title bare.
- **No "Physics" in the title.** The buyer has to know instantly that this is
  not the workshop.
- **Do not stack construction signals.** "Blueprint" plus "The Making Of" in the
  same lockup is two build cues together. This is why Blueprint was dropped
  rather than combined.
- **Do not pick for search.** They type how does a reformer work, or why do
  springs feel different. The title carries story; the plain language goes in
  the H1 subline and meta description.

### The series

Chair and Tower editions are logical follow-ons and ship into a ladder that
already exists: this feeds PP101, and the chair and tower editions feed PP102.

**The series does not need one word running through it.** "The Making of a
Chair" would imply a chair was designed, which is not true. That is fine. The
workshops are numbered rather than themed, and the follow-ons genuinely are
different products, since they carry no design story. Name them when they exist
rather than letting a hypothetical product line veto the real one. Reformer
Blueprint is available as a series name for the follow-ons if a shared label
turns out to be wanted later.

**Open naming question:** PP102 covers chair and Cadillac, but the follow-on was
described as chair and tower. Those are different pieces of equipment to a lot
of teachers. Pick the word that matches PP102 and use it consistently, or the
ladder gets fuzzy exactly where it should be obvious.

### Rejected, and why

Kept as a record so these do not get relitigated.

| Rejected | Reason |
|----------|--------|
| Exploded View: The Reformer | Requires explanation, and a title that needs explaining cannot carry an ad |
| Design Notes: The Reformer | Mouthful |
| Introduction to Reformer Design | "Introduction to" signals beginner exactly where the product needs to feel specialist, collides with "101," and reads as a course in how to design reformers |
| Reformer by Design | Rejected outright |
| Reformer Teardown | Best engineering register of the set, but module 8 is wear and maintenance, so a meaningful number of people would read it as a repair course |
| Reformer Mechanics | Safe, clear, and forgettable |
| Reformer Blueprint | Runner-up, and the closest call. Punchier and more concrete, but weaker on story, and it carries a real DIY-search risk that the winning title does not. Held in reserve as a possible series name for the chair and tower editions |

## Offer terms

| Item | Decision |
|------|----------|
| Evergreen price | $69 on the site |
| Promotional price | $39 for 72 hours, inside the email sequence only |
| Urgency lever | Price only. All content is always included |
| Content bonus | Deliberately deferred. Can be added later without restructuring the offer |
| Order bump at PP101 checkout | $39, permanently |
| Enforcement mechanism | **Deferred.** Logistics to be worked out later |

### Framing rules

- **Do not call $39 a founding price.** The window recurs for every new
  subscriber, so "founding" becomes a lie. Tie the discount to the subscriber
  being new, not the product being new: "you just joined the list, so you have
  three days at $39." That is true forever and works on subscriber 4,000 as well
  as on the first 300.
- **The public page shows $69 only.** No "regularly $69, today $39." The moment
  the public page names the discount, $69 stops reading as real to anyone who
  browses. The $39 lives only in the email path.
- **$39 is the permanent order-bump price** at PP101 checkout. One number, no
  contradiction, and nobody ever feels punished for the timing of their
  purchase.
- **State the CEC count on both sides of the ladder** once confirmed. "1 CEC
  here, X in 101" makes the ladder countable instead of a value claim that has
  to be argued.

## Email sequence

Four emails over three days, running after nurture email 5 (the origin story).
This adapts Nikki's mini sales sequence structure. Two of her four slots do not
fit this product.

| Day | Email | Job |
|-----|-------|-----|
| 1 | **Cart open: one change, three effects** | Lead with the trickle-down hook. You gear out one notch and three other things move. Open the offer, name the $39 price and the 72-hour deadline |
| 2 | **Five questions about your own reformer** | Replaces Nikki's case study slot. An interactive self-quiz. Miss two and you buy |
| 3 | **The springs you have not looked at** | Wear, plastic set, coatings, inspection. The email that reaches studio owners, who buy for staff |
| 4 | **Cart close: and what this is not** | Sell the vision of not guessing at the machine, then say plainly that this does not cover the body and 101 is where that lives |

### Why the case study slot got replaced

There is no client transformation story to tell. The product has no body in it
and no students yet. The self-quiz does three jobs at once: it disarms the "I
already know my machine" objection without arguing, it is nearly impossible not
to open because it is about equipment they touch daily, and it segments the list
by score in Kit so day 3 can hit low scorers harder and leave high scorers
alone.

### Why "I hate math" is not the objection here

It is the top objection for the workshop, but not for this product. These people
opted into a spring calculator, so they have already self-selected as the
technical segment. The real objection is **"I already know my machine,"** which
is what email 2 exists to handle. Math reassurance goes in the subtitle, not in
an email.

## Hook and positioning

| Asset | Line | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| Paid ad, primary | Trickle-down: you gear out one notch, three other things move, and nobody told you which | Corrects a habit rather than a fact. She does this weekly, so classes she already taught were affected, and the fault is clearly in her training rather than in her |
| Paid ad, A/B | Pulleys do halve the load, but not the way you think | Teachers say this out loud to clients, and being wrong in public is a sharper itch than being wrong in private |
| Sales page headline | You own more machine than you are using | Opportunity rather than error. Concrete version: "There are N things you can adjust on a reformer. Most teachers use four" |
| Inside the product | Friction is not the problem you think it is | Relief does not sell, but it buys belief for everything after it |
| Organic reach only | Classical vs contemporary: physics takes no side | Everyone else in that argument has a side. High reach, and real value for teachers who trained on one and now teach the other. Keep it out of paid, since tribal traffic converts badly at the top of a funnel |

Note that "springs are not one weight" is already spoken for by the calculator.
This product needs the next misconception, not that one.
