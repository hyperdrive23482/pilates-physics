# Hook Library

Fill-in-the-blank opening lines for reels, carousels, and captions. Use it when
brainstorming Instagram posts: pick a topic, pick a hook, fill the blank.

[story-framework.md](story-framework.md) tells you **what order to tell a story
in**, and says slide 1 of a carousel and the first seconds of a reel are the open
loop. This file is **the bank of loops to pick from**.

Source: adapted from two of Daria Medric's carousels (Instagram, 2026), written
for doctors building a personal brand: "Doctors: steal these hooks if you want
your reels to explode" (the 20 hooks), "5 reels every doctor can film this
week" (the five formats) and "The doctors making the most money online are
rarely posting the most educational content" (the judgment rule). The structure transfers well: an expert with years of hands-on
reps, talking to people who trust them but do not see what they see. Several of
the lines do not transfer as written. See
[What we do not take from the source](#what-we-do-not-take-from-the-source).

The source's core rule, which is ours too:

> Don't copy the topic. Copy the psychology.

Your knowledge is probably not the problem. The packaging is.

## Explain the physics, show the judgment

Educational content makes people smarter. Positioning content makes people
choose you. You can explain spring physics for years, but if a viewer cannot
answer "why should I learn this from her?", the account is a free physics
textbook, not a name people remember.

This is the PEACE reframe applied to social: physics is the engine, not the
product. See [peace-framework.md](peace-framework.md). A post that only explains
how a spring works is the engine with nothing attached. The post earns its keep
when it shows what you **do** with that knowledge for the body in front of you.

| Explanation only | With judgment |
|------------------|---------------|
| Explains how springs work | Explains how you decide which spring, and why |
| Shares a spring fact | Shares the call you would make with it for a specific client |
| Talks about the equipment | Talks about the teacher's problem the equipment causes |
| Chases views | Builds recognition: the same lens, post after post |
| Gets followers | Gets people who want to learn the way you teach |

The left column is a description of a post, not of a teacher. Do not turn this
table into public copy about "forgettable" instructors. That lands across, not
up. See the guardrails.

**This does not mean stop teaching physics.** Spring School, Pop Quiz and Weight
Stack posts stay. Add one layer: end the explanation with the teaching move it
leads to. "Here's why the red spring feels lighter for a tall client" becomes
"...so here's what I'd change for them."

**The trust check.** Before a post ships, ask which of these a viewer would think
after watching. If the answer is none, it is an explanation only. Add the
judgment.

1. "That's exactly my problem." (It names a real teaching situation.)
2. "I like how this teacher thinks." (It shows the reasoning, not just the fact.)
3. "This is who I'd want to learn from." (It shows a lens they do not have yet.)
4. "I'm going to remember this name." (It sounds like the last ten posts, in a
   good way.)

People do not choose a teacher trainer by how many facts they can explain. They
choose someone they believe can help them.

## Who is "they" in the blank

The source talks to patients. We have two audiences, and the same hook reads
differently for each. Decide before filling the blank.

| Audience | Who | The blank usually holds |
|----------|-----|-------------------------|
| Instructors (default) | ICP 1, the Curious Practitioner. See [icp-profiles.md](icp-profiles.md) | A spring setting, a cue, a modification, a client body type, a piece of equipment |
| Clients and enthusiasts | People who take classes | An exercise, a sensation ("too easy," "too hard"), a machine, a studio choice |

Where the source says "patients," read "clients." Where it says "doctor," read
"instructor" or "teacher training."

## The five triggers

The strongest hooks set off one of these in the viewer. Every hook below is
tagged with the one it pulls. When a brainstorm list leans hard on one trigger,
swap a few out so the feed does not hit the same nerve every post.

| # | Trigger | What the viewer thinks | Pilates Physics version |
|---|---------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| T1 | Doing it wrong | "I might be doing this wrong." | "I might be giving the wrong setting." Aim it at the setting, never the teacher. See the guardrails |
| T2 | Self-recognition | "Wait... is this me?" | "That is my tall client." / "That is my footwork." |
| T3 | Spend | "I was about to spend money on that." | A reformer, a certification, new springs, a home machine |
| T4 | Untold | "Nobody told me this." | "My teacher training never mentioned this." This is our home trigger, the PEACE Problem beat |
| T5 | Curiosity | "I need to know what happens next." | The open loop from [story-framework.md](story-framework.md) |

back-to-back posts.

## Five formats you can film this week

A hook is one line. A format is the whole reel: the hook plus what the body of
the video has to do to pay it off. These five need no trends, no dancing and no
complicated editing. Talk to the phone the way you talk to a client at the
footbar.

**The rule underneath all five: your next reel is hiding in a conversation you
already had.** A question a client asked, a look on a trainee's face when the
spring changed, something you explained twice this week. You already answer
these every day. That is the content.

| # | Format | What the body has to do | Pilates Physics examples | Hooks it pairs with |
|---|--------|-------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | The question I get almost every day | Open with a real question you keep getting, word for word. Then answer it exactly like you would at the reformer. Not a lecture. Make it sound like the person is standing next to the machine | "Do springs wear out?" / "Why does footwork feel harder on this reformer?" / "Is a red spring the same on every brand?" | 4, 20 |
| 2 | I would never... | A short list (three or four things) you would not do. The list is not the point. **Explain why** for each one. That is where people start to see how you think | "3 things I'd never do when setting springs for a tall client." / "3 things I'd never trust on a spec sheet, as someone who designed a reformer." | 1, 17, 18 |
| 3 | You think it's ___, but I'd look at ___ | Start with something the viewer already believes, then give them a reason to stay. Agree that the belief makes sense first, then show what you would check | "You think your client needs a heavier spring. I'd look at their body weight first." / "You think the exercise is too easy. I'd check the gear bar." | 5, 19 |
| 4 | What I notice immediately as a ___ | Let people borrow your trained eye. Clients and newer teachers rarely get to see a reformer the way a designer or teacher trainer sees it. Show them | "What I notice first when someone asks me which home reformer to buy." / "What gets my attention when a client says 'my knees feel weird in footwork.'" | 7, 13, 16 |
| 5 | If this were me, here's what I'd do first | **Make the situation specific.** A person, a body, a number, a deadline. Then the first step, not the whole plan | "If I were 5'2" and every class put me on the same springs as a 6' client, here's what I'd ask for." / "If I had 30 days before my comprehensive exam, here's what I'd study first." / "If my client's footwork felt 'easy' but they were shaking by set two, here's what I'd change." | 8, 15 |

Format 2 is the one most likely to break the voice rules. "I would never" is a
judgment, so keep it on yourself, a setting, or a piece of equipment, never on
what another teacher does. The guardrails below apply.

Formats 1 and 4 are the safest place to start, because they are just you
talking about what you see.

## What we do not take from the source

The source was written for doctors, where "stop doing this immediately" can be
about real harm. Ours is not, and our voice rules forbid most of the heat those
lines run on. Check every hook against these before it ships.

- **Punch up, never across.** "The biggest mistake I see people make" and "the
  trend I wish people would stop copying" land on a person. Point the heat at a
  setting, a piece of advice, or equipment marketing instead. That is why hooks 4,
  10 and 17 are rewritten above. See "punch up, never across" in
  [voice-and-messaging.md](voice-and-messaging.md).
- **No fear of harm.** Do not imply a spring setting injured someone, or that a
  teacher is hurting clients. "Stop doing this immediately" becomes "try this
  first."
- **No fear of the math.** A hook can promise a surprise. It cannot promise that
  the physics is hard.
- **Do not imply the training failed them.** "Your training told you ___" is a
  setup for the reason behind the setting, not a takedown of the training.
- **No all caps for emphasis.** The source shouts NEVER, NOT and THIS. We let the
  idea carry it.
- **No invented quotes or numbers.** Hooks 9 and 14 need a real number of years
  and a real client line. Pull them from [story-bank.md](story-bank.md).
- **Normal copy rules still apply.** No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no title
  case.

## How to brainstorm with this

1. Start from a real conversation: a question a client or trainee asked, or
   something you explained this week. If there is none, pick the topic (a
   spring, an exercise, a body type, a piece of equipment).
2. Note the Instagram planner category it belongs to, and pick the audience:
   instructors or clients.
3. Pick one of the five formats. It decides what the body of the reel does.
4. Try the topic in three or four hooks with different triggers. Keep the one
   that sounds most like something you would say out loud in class.
5. Check it against the guardrails above, then run the trust check from
   [Explain the physics, show the judgment](#explain-the-physics-show-the-judgment).
   If it only explains, add the teaching move.
6. For a first-person story post, build the rest with
   [story-framework.md](story-framework.md). The hook is the loop, and the
   Realization has to pay it off.
