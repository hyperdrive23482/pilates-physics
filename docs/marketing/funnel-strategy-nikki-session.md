### Core marketing message ("root belief")

- The foundational one-liner agreed upon: **"Springs are not one weight"** — every piece of content and every product must tie back to this
- This means every person in a class is experiencing a different load, making it essential for teachers to understand spring mechanics
- The value proposition for clients: **"When your clients feel it in the right places, they'll keep coming back"**
- Strength training context was noted as relevant (perimenopause, bone density, sarcopenia) but Pilates physics is positioned as its own lane

### Funnel structure overview

| Stage | What it is |
|-------|------------|
| Lead magnet | Spring calculator |
| Follow-up mini content | Footwork application (video or PDF) |
| Nurture sequence | 5 emails introducing philosophy and building trust |
| Mini sales sequence | 4 emails over ~3 days selling a tripwire product (body weight webinar) |
| Main list | Ongoing content |
| Main offer | Pilates Physics 101 live workshop |

### Lead magnet: spring calculator

- Interactive tool showing how much Pilates springs from major manufacturers weigh at any extension
- Intentionally not fully explanatory — meant to create curiosity, not solve the whole problem
- Follow-up content idea: short video or PDF showing a single, concrete application to footwork (high-spring exercise, stark difference between short and tall clients)
- Give one quick win ("you can apply this in the class you teach in 10 minutes"), then hint at more (e.g., Pilates Physics class) without a hard sell

### Nurture sequence (5 emails)

1. **POV / root belief** — "Here's how springs are usually taught and why it's incomplete"
2. **The enemy** — The belief that the same spring settings work for everyone; "if you don't feel it, you're doing it wrong"
3. **The misdiagnosis** — You think the problem is how you explain things; don't reveal the real answer yet
4. **The real diagnosis** — What is actually going on (loads, body weight, height); make sense of their experience
5. **Origin story + scar reel** — How Kaleen got here, what was gotten wrong, what is known now; builds trust and authority

Keep each email to one idea only; resist combining problem and solution in the
same email.

### Mini sales sequence (4 emails, ~3 days)

- **Day 1 — Mini cart open:** Introduce the tripwire (body weight/dimensions mini course or webinar); offer a limited-time discount or bonus
- **Day 2 — Case study:** Longer-form testimonial telling a client transformation story in their words and yours; optionally add quick testimonials at the bottom
- **Day 3 — Objection email ("I hate math"):** Address the biggest objection — reassure that math is only used to illustrate concepts, not performed while teaching
- **Day 4 — Cart close:** Sell the vision (what teaching looks and feels like after this), reinforce urgency and discount deadline

Mechanics:

- Use a countdown timer plugin on the landing page to enforce the 72-hour window
- Price the tripwire higher than intended so the discount feels meaningful
- The testimonial email can swap positions with the objection email — that is the flexible slot

### Tripwire product: body weight mini course

- Bridges the gap between the free spring calculator and the full workshop
- Topic: body weight (and height/limb length) affects load in ways teachers weren't taught — "more springs isn't always harder"
- Should deliver one clear, immediately applicable win

### Immediate plan for 300 new subscribers

- ~300 new subscribers came in recently (of ~500 total); have only received one email about the calculator so far
- Workshop cart closes next Wednesday
- Recommended approach:
  1. Send a VIP early-adopter email framed as appreciation, offering an exclusive discount or bonus (e.g., a live group call) not available publicly
  2. Follow with a value email (e.g., footwork spring tip) with a soft PS sell
  3. Send a cart close email with urgency, offer details, and testimonials in the PS
