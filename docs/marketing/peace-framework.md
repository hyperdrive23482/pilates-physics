# PEACE Messaging Framework

PEACE is Donald Miller's five-beat messaging tool: **P**roblem, **E**mpathy,
**A**nswer, **C**hange, **E**nd Result. Each beat is a short, repeatable
soundbite that positions the customer as the hero and Pilates Physics as the
guide. Use these as-is, or as the backbone you expand from.

The master set is written for **ICP 1, The Curious Practitioner** (see
[icp-profiles.md](icp-profiles.md)). Short alt hooks for ICP 2 and ICP 3 follow.

For the general principles behind these beats (survival framing, cognitive load,
the controlling idea, the three silent questions), see
[soundbite-principles.md](soundbite-principles.md). The Pilates Physics
application of those principles is in the last section of this file.

**The reframe.** Physics is not the product, it is the engine. The promise is
that you can personalize class for every body: the cues, the language, the
options, and the settings. The physics is the why underneath that makes it
credible and repeatable. Sell the personalization, prove it with the physics.

## Master one-liner (the elevator version)

> Most instructors were handed the settings and the script, but never the why.
> Pilates Physics gives you the why, so you can build every class around the body
> in front of you, not the model in the training manual.

Use this as the single-sentence description of the brand: bios, meta
descriptions, "what is this" openers.

**Standalone tagline:** Powered by physics. Use it as a lockup under the logo, at
the close of a CTA section, or as a sign-off. Keep it out of the one-liner, where
it reads as repetitive.

## The five beats

### P: Problem

> "The same setting is not the same workout. Every body meets that spring
> differently."

**Support line:** Set the standard spring for the room and a taller body and a
shorter body feel two different loads. Rote settings hide that from you, so you
can't see who is working too hard and who is barely working at all.

**Built from:** "Shorter legs may need more spring tension than longer legs
because they cannot stretch the springs as far." / "what works for my classmates
just doesn't work for me."

**Weight:** Miller puts the Problem at roughly half of the whole messaging
campaign. This is the beat to get right before any other. The key insight: the
problem is not that everyone gets the same class. It is that the same setting is a
different workout for every body, and rote teaching leaves you blind to it. Keep
this front and center.

### E: Empathy

> "Memorizing the settings and the script is exactly where you start.
> Personalizing them is where you grow."

**Support line:** Learning the settings and the script by heart is not a shortcut
or a gap. It is the foundation every good teacher builds on. Personalizing them is
the next stage.

**Built from:** the corrected empathy stance. Honor the journey. The training was
the right starting line, not a failure.

### A: Answer

> "Pilates Physics shows you why the same setting lands differently on every body,
> and gives you the cues to adjust for it."

**Support line:** It explains why bodies feel different loads, shows how each
setting changes that load, then hands you practical cues and options to change
what you teach. No math background required.

**Built from:** "You took an intimidating topic (Physics!) and made it so
understandable." / "It's helped me to develop quick questions to ask them for
feedback when we get started."

**Conversion note:** Miller's Answer beat is the rope you throw into the hole, and
it should name what the reader can actually buy. The line above describes the
benefit, which is right for the top of a page. Lower down, on a sales page or
CTA, make the Answer concrete and name the product:

> "The answer is the Pilates Physics workshop: a live session, plus the recording,
> that teaches you the physics behind the settings and how to teach with it."

Swap in the specific course when relevant (Pilates Physics 101 for Reformer, 102
for Chair and Cadillac).

### C: Change

> "Stop regurgitating memorized settings and scripts. Start offering personalized
> options that empower every student."

**Support line:** The lever isn't just the spring. It's the footbar and body
position, the cue you give, the words you use with a client, how you run a mixed
room. Small, deliberate changes that add up to a far more effective workout for
every body.

**Built from:** "My go to has always been change the springs. Now I will be trying
other things first." / "critical thinking instead of just regurgitating."

**Note:** Miller treats Change as the optional beat, the "icing on the cake." It
earns its place here because the change students actually voiced goes well beyond
the machine: it is a shift in how they cue, the language they use, and how they
run the room. Because it overlaps a little with the Answer beat and the client
language in the End Result, cut it on a page that already leans on those, and keep
it where it adds lift.

### E: End Result

> "Every student gets a class built for their body, not the model in your training
> manual."

**Support line:** Clients feel the difference because the class was built around
them. And they trust you, because you can tell them what their body is feeling and
why.

**Built from:** "I feel empowered to make more equipment modifications to better
fit MY body's needs." / "the more I understand about how my height and weight
affect my practice, the more I get from it."

## Alt hooks for ICP 2 and ICP 3

These swap only the **Problem** and **Empathy** beats. The Answer, Change, and
End Result stay shared with the master set.

### ICP 2: Seasoned Educator / Studio Owner

- **P:** "You already personalize for every body by instinct. Your instructors
  are still teaching the settings and the script."
- **E:** "A decade of intuition earned you this. Now you can put the why into
  words and teach it to your team."

**Built from:** "encouraging my more junior instructors to take these courses." /
"help me explain the physics in pilates movements better to my students."

### ICP 3: Aspiring / Student-Practitioner

- **P:** "You feel abandoned in class because the standard setting was never a
  workout built for your body."
- **E:** "Wanting to understand how your body meets the machine isn't asking for
  too much. It's how you finally get what you came for."

**Built from:** "Short and curvy isn't the norm... I feel abandoned in them at
times." / "the more I understand about how my height and weight affect my
practice, the more I get from it."

## Where to deploy each beat

| Beat | Best used for |
|------|---------------|
| One-liner | Bios, meta descriptions, "what is this" intros, ad primary text |
| Problem | Hero headline, email subject lines, social hooks (lead with the pain) |
| Empathy | The first body line after the hero, opening of a nurture email |
| Answer | Sub-headline, the "here's what it is" section, CTA lead-in |
| Change | Bullet lists of what shifts, before/after framing, mid-page |
| End Result | Closing CTA section, testimonial lead-in, the "imagine" close |

**Rule of thumb:** lead with Problem or Empathy to earn attention, then move to
Answer and Change, and close on End Result. Do not open with the Answer.

## Applying Don Miller's soundbite principles to Pilates Physics

These translate the general ideas in
[soundbite-principles.md](soundbite-principles.md) into decisions for this brand.

### Our controlling idea (the one thing)

**Give every body the class it actually needs, powered by physics.** Every piece
of copy should ladder up to this. The promise is personalization: the cues, the
language, the options, and the settings that fit the body in front of you. Physics
is the why underneath, expressed through a few apparatus angles:

- **Reformer** (Pilates Physics 101)
- **Chair and Cadillac** (Pilates Physics 102)
- **Advanced / future apparatus** (Pilates Physics 201 and beyond)

These are sub-angles of the one idea, not separate messages. Anything that does
not ladder under "personalize the class, powered by the physics behind the
equipment" is a different business, not a new headline.

### What "survival asset" means for our ICP

Our hero is a teacher, so survival here is professional, not literal. Pilates
Physics is a survival asset because personalizing with confidence helps her:

- Keep clients and stand out, instead of blending in with every other teacher who
  runs the same script for the whole room.
- Teach safely and adapt for bodies that do not fit the standard, which protects
  both the client and her reputation. The same setting is a different load on
  every body, and not seeing that is a real risk.
- Feel credible instead of exposed, especially in front of experienced clients or
  when a setting "just does not work" for someone.

Copy should connect the physics back to one of these stakes, not treat it as
knowledge for its own sake.

### Keep it low cognitive load

The homepage header and any first line has one job: pique curiosity with a zero
cognitive load message. Lead with the Problem soundbite ("the same setting is not
the same workout"), not with an explanation of what physics we cover. Save the
nuance (free body diagrams, force vectors, the math) for the enlightenment phase,
once she is already reading. Remember: clarity wins, and if you confuse, you lose.

### The three silent questions, answered for a Pilates Physics page

A visitor decides in about five seconds. A landing page must answer:

1. **What do you offer?** A live Pilates Physics workshop (plus the recording)
   that teaches you to personalize class for every body, with the physics behind
   it. Name the specific course.
2. **How will it make my life better?** You will build every class around the body
   in front of you: the right cues, options, and settings, and you can explain the
   why to your clients.
3. **What do I need to do to buy it?** One clear next step: register now, or join
   the waitlist when a cohort is closed. Scarcity (cohort caps, early-bird
   windows) is fine and helps.

### Stay disciplined and repeat

Once these soundbites are set, work at least one into every page, email, and post,
and ideally all five in longer pieces. Repeat them until students start saying
them back to you. That is the signal they are working.
