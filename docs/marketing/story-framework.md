# Story Framework

How to sequence a first-person story in a newsletter or a social post. Use it
when the piece is narrative and told in your own voice, not when it is a landing
page.

[peace-framework.md](peace-framework.md) tells you **what to claim**.
[website-structure.md](website-structure.md) tells you **where a claim goes on a
page**. This file tells you **what order to tell it in** when the piece is a
story.

Source: distilled from Matt Gray's "How to become a storytelling genius" carousel
(Instagram, 2026). The structure is useful. The delivery is not ours. See
[What we do not take from the source](#what-we-do-not-take-from-the-source)
before you use anything from it.

## The five lines, mapped to PEACE

The narrative order is not the claim order. Same beats, told as a sequence rather
than stated as soundbites.

| Line | The job | Nearest PEACE beat |
|------|---------|--------------------|
| 1. Mirror | Where she is right now | Empathy |
| 2. Friction | Why she is stuck | Problem |
| 3. Realization | The thing nobody told her | Answer |
| 4. Shift | What changed for you | Change |
| 5. Invitation | The next step | End Result and CTA |

Two differences from the page order worth noticing. The story opens on **Empathy,
not Problem**, because a stranger will not accept your diagnosis until they
believe you have seen their situation. And the **Shift** line, which on a page is
the optional Change beat, is the one that carries the whole narrative payload
here. It is also the line most likely to break the piece. See
[The hero rule](#the-hero-rule).

## The five lines

### 1. Mirror

**Job:** show her you have seen her actual Tuesday. One specific, recognizable
moment, not a category of person.

**Craft notes:**

- Specific beats general. "You reach for the springs" lands. "Many instructors
  struggle with personalization" does not.
- Reach into [customer-language.md](customer-language.md) first. A student's own
  words are a better mirror than anything written from scratch.
- Present tense, second person, one moment.
- Never diagnose here. Naming the problem in line 1 skips straight to being
  right at her, which is the thing that makes people stop reading.

**Our fill:** "Someone in your class says the spring feels too heavy. You take
one off. It is now too light. You put it back and tell her to go slower."

**Built from:** "My go to has always been change the springs." /
"what works for my classmates just doesn't work for me."

**What breaks it:** a mirror she does not recognize. If she reads it and thinks
"that is not me," nothing after it can recover.

### 2. Friction

**Job:** name why the obvious fix does not work. This is the Problem beat, and
per [soundbite-principles.md](soundbite-principles.md) it carries roughly half the
weight of the whole message.

**Craft notes:**

- The friction is never that she is doing it wrong. It is that the tool she was
  handed is too coarse. Honor the training. See the Do-not list in
  [voice-and-messaging.md](voice-and-messaging.md).
- One friction per piece. Two ideas here and the loop stops pulling.
- This is where the master Problem soundbite goes, in her situation rather than
  as a claim.

**Our fill:** "A whole spring is a big jump, and the same setting is not the same
workout. Set the standard for the room and the tall client and the short client
are doing two different amounts of work. Nothing on the chart tells you which is
which."

**What breaks it:** implying her training failed her, or that there is a trick
she should already know.

### 3. Realization

**Job:** the turn. The thing that was true the whole time and nobody said out
loud. This is the line people screenshot.

**Craft notes:**

- It should feel obvious the second after you read it, and invisible the second
  before. That gap is the whole effect.
- Keep it to one sentence at zero cognitive load. The explanation goes in the
  line after it, not inside it.
- This is the Answer beat as insight, not as product. Do not name the workshop
  here. Naming what you sell in line 3 collapses the story into an ad.

**Our fill:** "A spring is a slope, not a number. What it costs her depends on how
far it stretches, so the footbar, the gear, and where her body starts change the
load as much as the spring does."

**Built from:** "Shorter legs may need more spring tension than longer legs
because they cannot stretch the springs as far."

**What breaks it:** a realization that is really a feature list. If it could be
followed by "and that is why our course covers," it is not a realization.

### 4. Shift

**Job:** what changed for you once you saw it. This earns the guide role. It is
the shortest line in the piece.

**Craft notes:**

- Two or three sentences. On a page it can be one line. The moment it becomes a
  paragraph, the reader is watching you instead of herself.
- Past to present, concrete and small. What you stopped doing, what you do now.
- It must be true and lived. Do not manufacture a transformation. Material for
  this line lives in [story-bank.md](story-bank.md).

**Our fill:** _not yet written._ The Shift line needs real material from
[story-bank.md](story-bank.md), which is currently a scaffold. Until it is
filled in, run pieces on the other four lines rather than inventing a story.

**The shape it should take:** "I taught the chart for years and told people to go
slower when it did not fit them. Now I change the footbar before I touch a
spring, and I can tell the client why."

**What breaks it:** length, numbers, and credentials. Revenue figures and
name-dropping are the failure mode of the source framework and are banned here.

### 5. Invitation

**Job:** the next step, arriving as the natural consequence of lines 1 through 4
rather than as a pivot.

**Craft notes:**

- If the CTA feels like a change of subject, the story did not open a loop the
  CTA can close. Fix the story, not the button.
- One ask. Not "reply, and also subscribe, and also register."
- Which ask depends on temperature, and follows the two-CTA funnel rule in
  [website-structure.md](website-structure.md): cold audiences get Springs 101,
  warm audiences get workshop registration.

**Our fill (cold):** "Springs 101 is the short version of that idea, free, in
your inbox."

**Our fill (warm):** name the specific course, Pilates Physics 101 for Reformer
or 102 for Chair and Cadillac, and what she gets.

**What breaks it:** an ask that does not answer the loop you opened. If the story
was about the footbar and the CTA is a general newsletter signup, the loop stays
open and she leaves with it.

## Open loops, and holding the tension

An unanswered question is uncomfortable enough that people stay to resolve it.
That is the mechanism behind the curiosity phase in
[soundbite-principles.md](soundbite-principles.md), applied to a body of copy
rather than a header. Miller's point is that curiosity is the price of admission
to enlightenment. The loop is how you keep paying it past the first line.

**How it works in practice.** Open a question in the first line or two. Do not
answer it until the Realization. Everything in between is tension the reader is
carrying for you.

**Opener patterns, in our voice:**

- Contradiction: "The setting was right. It was still the wrong load for her."
- Withheld cause: "There is a reason the same spring feels heavy to one client
  and light to another, and it is not strength."
- Delayed correction: "I gave the same cue for two years before I understood what
  I was actually asking people to do."
- Named unknown: "There is a dial on the reformer that changes the load more than
  the springs do, and almost nobody adjusts it on purpose."

**Three rules:**

1. **Close every loop you open,** inside the piece or with the CTA. An unclosed
   loop reads as a bait headline and costs trust, which is the one thing this
   brand runs on.
2. **One loop per piece.** Two competing questions and neither pulls.
3. **The loop has to be true.** Every pattern above is a promise that something
   specific is coming. If the payoff is thinner than the setup, do not run it.

**Subject lines.** This is where loops earn the most. Compare the existing
`nurture-springs-101.md` subject lines: "Do this before you read the primer"
is a loop, "A red spring is not a single weight" is a contradiction. Both work
because the email delivers on them.

## The hero rule

**The reader is the hero. You are the guide.** That is StoryBrand, it is the
basis of [website-structure.md](website-structure.md), and it does not bend for
this framework.

The five-line structure has a built-in risk: line 4 is explicitly your
transformation. Told long, it makes you the hero of a story she is only watching.
Told short, it is exactly Miller's Empathy beat, the guide standing at the edge
of the hole saying "I have been down there, and I have helped people out." See
the hole metaphor in [soundbite-principles.md](soundbite-principles.md).

**The check.** Read the finished piece and ask what the last line leaves her
thinking. If it is "she really knows her stuff," the Shift is too long. If it is
"I could do that on Tuesday," it is the right length.

**Two practical constraints:**

- The Shift line stays under three sentences, always.
- Lines 1, 2, 3, and 5 are about her. Only line 4 is about you. If your ratio is
  worse than one in five, rebalance before you ship.

## Making the CTA inevitable

The story opens a loop. The lead magnet closes it. That is the whole logic, and
it is why the transitional CTA in [website-structure.md](website-structure.md)
works the way it does.

A CTA reads as a jolt when it answers a question the reader was not asking. It
reads as inevitable when it is the obvious next move on the tension you just
built. Practically:

- Write the Realization first, then pick the CTA that extends it. Not the other
  way around.
- The gap between the Realization and the Invitation should be one idea wide. If
  the story was about spring stretch and the offer is a full workshop on load,
  bridge it with a line, or offer Springs 101 instead.
- Springs 101 is the default close for anything cold or narrative, because a
  story earns attention, not commitment. Registration is the close when the
  reader is already warm.

## Where to deploy

| Channel | How the five lines land |
|---------|-------------------------|
| Newsletter body | All five, in order. This is the format the framework was built for |
| Newsletter subject line | The loop only. Usually a compressed Friction or a contradiction |
| Instagram carousel | One line per slide, five to seven slides. Slide 1 is the loop, not the Mirror |
| Instagram caption | Mirror, Realization, Invitation. Drop Friction and Shift, there is no room |
| Reel or video hook | The loop, held for the first fifteen seconds before the Realization |
| Blog post intro | Mirror and Friction only, then hand off to the body. The Realization becomes the post |

**Compression rule:** when you cannot fit five, keep Mirror, Realization, and
Invitation. Those three still make a story. Keeping Shift and cutting Mirror
makes an ad about you.

## What we do not take from the source

The structure is sound. The delivery is off-brand in ways that matter, so nothing
transfers verbatim. [voice-and-messaging.md](voice-and-messaging.md) is the final
word, and it rules out:

- **Revenue and status flexing.** Income figures, client name-drops, follower
  counts. Our credibility comes from teaching something true, not from scale.
- **Exclamation marks and superlatives.** Confident, not hyped.
- **Em dashes.** Use a period or a comma.
- **The "22x" statistic.** The claim that people remember stories many times
  better than facts is widely repeated and thinly sourced. It can motivate this
  file. It must not appear in Pilates Physics copy as a fact.
- **Competitor framing.** No "every other teacher" contrast that punches down at
  the people we are trying to teach.
- **Manufactured vulnerability.** A story told for the structure rather than
  because it happened. This audience is experienced and will feel it.

## What this is grounded in, and what it is not

Every beat in [peace-framework.md](peace-framework.md) is built from one of 22
real post-workshop survey responses. This file is not, and neither is
[story-bank.md](story-bank.md). Founder-story material is brand voice, not voice
of customer. That is the same caveat flagged for progression in
[peace-framework.md](peace-framework.md), and it carries the same discipline:

- The Mirror and Friction lines **should** be sourced from
  [customer-language.md](customer-language.md), because that material exists.
- The Shift line cannot be, by definition. Keep it short, keep it true, and do
  not let it carry a claim the survey data does not support.
- If a story consistently lands, note it in [story-bank.md](story-bank.md) with
  what it was used for. Reply rates and saves are the only evidence this file
  will ever have.
