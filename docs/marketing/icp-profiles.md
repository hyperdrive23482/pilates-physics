# Ideal Customer Profiles

Three profiles, drawn from real Pilates Physics 101 and 102 survey responses.
When you write, pick one and speak to that person. Default to **ICP 1** unless
the piece has a reason to target another.

---

## ICP 1: The Curious Practitioner (PRIMARY)

**Who she is.** A thoughtful Pilates teacher, roughly 1 to 9 years in. She was
taught the "what" in her certification (which springs, which settings) but never
the "why" behind it. She is hungry to teach with intention instead of by rote,
and she suspects there is a whole layer of understanding she was never handed.

**What she struggles with**

- Her first move when a body does not fit the standard setting is to add or drop
  a spring, and hope. She knows that cannot be the only lever.
- She feels like she is "regurgitating" her training rather than thinking.
- She wants to serve varied bodies well, including her own, and the one-size
  cueing she learned does not always fit.
- Physics and math intimidate her. That fear is real and it is a barrier.

**What she wants**

- To understand the mechanics so she can adapt with confidence.
- Language to explain to clients what is actually happening and why.
- More tools than just the springs: footbar, gear, body position, load.
- To feel that the hard science is reachable, not gatekept.

**In her words**

> "My go to has always been change the springs. Now I will be trying other
> things first."

> "Knowing the why will help me with the real people to come to my classes."

> "I feel empowered to make more equipment modifications to better fit MY body's
> needs rather than just following along with a room full of completely different
> bodies than mine."

**How to speak to her.** Name the frustration of only having the rules, honor
that the rules were the right place to start, then promise the understanding that
turns rules into judgment. Disarm the math fear early. She wrote your richest
transformation language, so let her voice lead.

## ICP 2: The Seasoned Educator / Studio Owner

**Who she is.** 10+ years in. Often a master trainer, studio owner, or teacher
trainer, sometimes with a movement-science background. She already adapts for
every body by instinct. She takes the workshop as rigorous validation and, just
as importantly, as a resource she can push down to her junior instructors.

**What she struggles with**

- Her expertise is intuitive, so it is hard to teach. "Because I said so" does
  not train the next generation.
- Certification curricula are shallow, and she wants credible depth for her team.

**What she wants**

- Confirmation that her instincts are sound, plus precise language for them.
- Something rigorous she can confidently recommend to trainees and staff.

**In her words**

> "We will be recommending this to our teacher trainers, new instructors, AND our
> maintenance technician students!"

> "I will definitely be encouraging my more junior instructors to take these
> courses."

**How to speak to her.** Respect her experience first. The offer is not "learn
the basics," it is "put language to what you already know, and give your team the
rigor their cert skipped." She is a multiplier and a referral source, so her
lower emotional heat is worth a lot in reach.

## ICP 3: The Aspiring / Student-Practitioner

**Who she is.** Not yet teaching (in teacher training) or a devoted student.
Often someone whose body does not fit the class "norm" (short, curvy, tall) and
who feels underserved by one-size cueing. She wants to understand how her own
body meets the equipment, both to get more from her practice and to prepare to
teach.

**What she struggles with**

- She feels abandoned in class because nothing was built for her body.
- She cannot self-advocate for equipment modifications, and support is thin
  (especially on the chair).

**What she wants**

- To understand how her height and weight change her experience on the machine.
- To get more from every session, and to build a foundation for teaching.

**In her words**

> "Short and curvy isn't the norm for my advanced classes, and I feel abandoned
> in them at times."

> "The more I understand about how my height and weight affect my practice, the
> more I enjoy and get from it."

**How to speak to her.** Validate that wanting to understand her own body is not
asking for too much. She is small in number but fiercely loyal (one asked to be
signed up for a future cert program on the spot), so treat her as a superfan.

## Watch-out (not an ICP)

The group-class instructor who wanted a live, do-it-while-you-learn practical
class and found the math "not applicable to teaching group classes." This is
where our only two low scores came from (an NPS 3 and a 7). Do not chase this
person, but let it shape expectations: copy should promise "the physics,
applied," not pure theory, and a live applied class may be a future product.

---

## The 5 messaging pillars

These themes showed up across almost every response, in every ICP. Any piece of
copy should ladder up to at least one of them.

1. **Teach me the WHY, not just the how.** A direct reaction against rote
   certification.
2. **Springs aren't the only dial.** The single most common behavior change:
   stop defaulting to spring swaps, use footbar, gear, position, and load.
3. **Every body is different.** Height, weight, and proportion. Serving diverse
   clients and their own non-standard body.
4. **Give me language to empower my clients.** From authority-by-rote to shared
   understanding, so clients can make informed choices.
5. **Physics scared me, and you made it click.** The fear-to-confidence arc, and
   the biggest barrier the messaging has to disarm.

## Data basis and one caveat

- **22 total responses:** 11 from Pilates Physics 101 (Reformer, May 2026) and 11
  from Pilates Physics 102 (Chair and Cadillac, July 2026).
- **Experience mix:** 10+ yrs 45% (10), 4-9 yrs 14% (3), 1-3 yrs 23% (5), under 1
  yr 9% (2), not an instructor 9% (2).
- **Satisfaction:** overwhelmingly 9s and 10s, with two low scores (a 3 and a 7)
  from the "wanted more hands-on, less math" camp described in the watch-out.

**Caveat.** This is feedback from people who already bought and cared enough to
reply. It tells us who loves Pilates Physics and what turns a buyer into a
superfan, which is exactly what an ICP is for. It does not capture people who
bounced or never bought. Read it as "who loves you," not "the whole market."
