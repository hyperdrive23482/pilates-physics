# Reformer Springs Across Contemporary Brands
**Topic:** Comparing spring profiles (light, medium, heavy) across Balanced Body, Stott, Align, and Peak; why conversion charts don't work

How do reformer springs compare across contemporary brands? Let's dig in. First, let's do a quick recap. Springs are not always one weight. You can't assign a single number to a spring. Springs get heavier the longer you stretch them. And the best way to represent this visually is with a graph. Okay, now that we've got that out of the way, let's jump into the actual brand comparisons. This is a graph of all the manufacturers lightest reformer spring. And you can see that across their travel, they're all within about 5 lbs of each other. Now, the 50% and 100% springs for these manufacturers start to vary between brands a little bit more, maybe about 10 lbs between them across the full spring extension. We start to notice that if we're switching between reformers, the most interesting graph is this one, the heavy springs. Now, there are two really interesting things that you can see from this graph. First, the initial tension of balance body start and a line are all very very similar. But as you stretch those springs, they get way more different. You can see those three lines getting wider as you travel on the graph. And then second, you can see that the peak spring is actually much heavier for initial tension, but by 36 in, it's one of the lighter springs of the four. So what does this mean practically? Well, the BB, the STO, and the Align Springs will all feel fairly similar for about 6 to 8 in of extension. But then once you get to 12 in of extension, they vary by almost 10 lbs. And that's practically really relevant because we are working in that 12 in of carriage extension range pretty often. Now, let's look at that peak spring. No matter how far the springs are stretched, the peak spring is always about 8 lbs heavier than the balanced body spring. So they have a K factor that's almost the same, but their initial tension is different. Now the peak heavy spring will feel heavier than a stop spring until about 20 in of extension. That's where those two lines cross where they feel about the same. And then for extension beyond 20 in, the peak spring will actually feel lighter. Now compare that to the align profile. The heavy peak spring will only feel heavier for the first 8 in of travel. At about 8 in, the peak and align heavy springs feel about the same. And then after that, the peak spring feels lighter. How do you apply this information in the real world? Now, if you teach on multiple brands at multiple studios, it's really worth knowing how the spring profiles are different. You can see that the light springs are nearly the same, practically interchangeable. The heavy springs are where the real difference lives. This analysis across brands shows that a simple spring conversion chart won't save you. Memorizing spring settings is not enough. What you actually need to know and understand is the intention of what you're teaching, how the specific equipment you're teaching on delivers load, and enough dialogue with your client to know when something isn't landing so that you can fix it.
