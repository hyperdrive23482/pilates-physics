# Adjustments That Change Spring Tension (Carousel, 2026-10-22)
**Format:** Carousel · **Posted:** 2026-10-22
**Topic:** Five common reformer adjustments (rope length, rope grip, footbar height, body distance to the shoulder rests, gear position) change spring resistance because they change how far the spring is stretched. 2. Most adjustments on the reformer impact spring resistance. That's because they change how far the spring is stretched. The more a spring is stretched, the heavier it is. The less a spring is stretched, the lighter it is.

3. 1) Rope Length. How long the ropes are when using them changes the spring resistance.
   LONGER ROPES: LIGHTER
   SHORTER ROPES: HEAVIER

4. 2) Rope Grip Position. Similar to rope length, where you hold the ropes changes spring resistance when using them.
   LONG LOOPS: LIGHTEST
   SHORT LOOPS: MEDIUM
   CHOKED UP: HEAVIEST

5. 3) Footbar height. When using a footbar that's on a pivot, raising or lowering the footbar changes the distance the footbar is to the carriage.
   LOWER: LIGHTER
   HIGHER: HEAVIER

6. 4) Body distance to shoulder rests. When using the ropes, such as supine arms in straps, seated arm work, or rowing.
   CLOSER TO ROPES: LIGHTER
   FURTHER FROM ROPES: HEAVIER

7. 5) Gear position. When using the footbar or ropes.
   GEAR 1: HEAVIEST
   GEAR 2: MEDIUM
   GEAR 3: LIGHTEST

8. Whether or not you mean to, it changes. When adjusting to accommodate different limb lengths, joint mobility, or available range under tension, be aware that your adjustment can inadvertently change the spring resistance the client feels. Sometimes it's okay, and sometimes it's no longer supporting your teaching intent.
