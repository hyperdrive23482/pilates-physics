# Pretensioning Springs
**Topic:** What the second row of spring hooks does; pre-tensioning shifts the spring's working range slightly

Have you ever wondered what this second row of spring hooks is on your gear bar? Lots of reformers have them. So, let's talk about how that impacts your spring tension. When you hook a spring onto the second row of spring hooks, you're essentially pre-tensioning the spring. This is a really nice way to slightly change the spring tension without making a completely different spring selection. So, if you look at the graph for the balance body red spring and say we're moving over a 6 inch range, when you pre-tension the spring, you're basically moving that 6 in over on the graph by an inch and a half or two inches. That distance is the same distance as the first row hook to the second row hook. You do the same distance shift on the graph. So, in this example, you can see that the resistance of the spring really only changes a pound or two. And sometimes that's all you need. And sometimes that's like you can barely feel it. Personally, I really only use the second row of spring hooks for the extra light springs where we're talking about um a very light resistance. So that extra little bit of stretch does impact the experience a little bit more than say pre-tensioning a very heavy spring. But that's really my personal preference in the style of teaching that I do. Now some reformers have three rows, some have two rows, some only have one row. It totally varies. I think this is more of a like cherry on top in terms of reformer design. I hardly ever use it.
