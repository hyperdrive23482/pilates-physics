# Pilates Springs Series — Part 5: Does Body Weight Change Spring Resistance?
**Topic:** Body weight does not change spring tension because of wheels; but body weight affects exercise experience through other factors like planks

Does body weight change the resistance of the springs? Let's do an experiment. For this experiment, we need a reformer with two heavy springs, a luggage scale, and a spring extension gauge to measure where 6 in of extension is. First, I'm sitting on the carriage and pulling 6 in out. And that luggage scale is right there between me and the rope. And it gets to about 18 pounds. Now, if I add my husband on the carriage, the luggage scale again reads about 18 pounds. The reason the resistance is the same whether you have one person or two people on the carriage is because the carriage has wheels. And the reason that body weight influences how hard it is to move something horizontal is because of friction. But when you have wheels with either bushings or bearings, friction becomes a tiny tiny tiny part of the equation and it's basically negligible in the Pilates setting. So, does body weight change spring tension? No. Yes, sometimes people of different body weights experience exercises differently. Totally normal. But it's not because the springs change their properties. Let's look at a plank for example. I'm going to take a few factors that could influence how they experience the plank out of it by saying they have the same height, they have the same spring settings, and they have the same reformer settings. So, gear position, foot bar position, and they're on the same reformer. But because they have different body weights, they might experience this exercise differently. It could be harder or easier. And one of the reasons for that is because in a plank you have to support your body weight. You have to lift it up. So you've got to get some force upward. And the other part is the body weight is actually pushing the carriage away from the foot bar. So you have to resist that motion. So when you're picking springs for a plank, there are a lot of factors to take into consideration. Um, and yes, the springs will be one of them, but not because springs inherently change properties or the carriage gets heavier by default because a person is heavier. It's because of all the other stuff that's happening. And a lot of times changing the springs will help get you to the intent you were aiming for with the exercise. But just understand, it's not because the carriage is all of a sudden harder to move.
