# Footbar vs Gearbar Adjustment for Footwork
**Topic:** Lowering the foot bar vs. gearing out as options for accommodating tall clients in footwork; trigonometry explains why both achieve similar spring extension

In my video about adjusting the gear position for tall people during footwork, I got a lot of comments asking if adjusting the foot bar lower is also a good option. And today I'm going to show you why it absolutely is. First, let's look at the max spring weight. So, this is when someone's knees are fully extended. So, you can see that in the foot bar position middle in gear 1, the springs stretch 19 and 1/2 in. and that is 88 pounds. In gear two, on the same foot bar position in the middle, the springs extend 16 in. So that's 77 lb with full knee extension. Now, interestingly, if I bring the carriage back to gear 1 and lower the foot bar to the low position, at full knee extension, the springs stretch 16 in as well. So 77 lbs. It's the same as gear position two with the foot bar in the middle position. But if you look at it, I mean the numbers happen to be the same, but is it actually the same? Well, let's look at the starting joint angle setup. So, first let's compare the foot bar in the middle position, gear one, which is on top, and gear two. You can see that when you gear out, my hip and knee angles open a little bit bigger. Now, if you compare gear one with the foot bar in the middle and gear one with the foot bar in the low position, you can see that my hip and knee angles actually open a little bit more. So, even more than just gearing out. But then, how do the springs stretch the same distance? Well, the answer is trigonometry. when you gear out but you don't change the foot bar, your heels are at the same height between version one, gear one and version two, gear two. So when you are at full knee extension, the angle of your legs above horizontal is actually the same whether you're in gear one or gear two. But if we compare gear one with foot bar high and gear one with foot bar low, when you're at full knee extension, your legs are actually at a different angle. So that very slight difference in leg angle takes up the slack between gearing out which is just moving horizontally and lowering the foot bar which moves it a little bit horizontal and a little bit down. So what does this mean for your teaching? Well, it means that both options either lowering the foot bar or gearing out one are valid choices for accommodating a client in footwork. Personally, I'd pick the one that interrupts your group flow the least. And because I think adjusting rope length is super annoying, especially in the middle of a class, I'm going to pick lowering the foot bar as much as I can because I don't want to mess with the ropes and I also want to maintain as much carriage travel as possible throughout the class. And in the next video, I'll show you what I mean by preserving the carriage travel within the frame.
