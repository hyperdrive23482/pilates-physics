Force a Push-Through Bar Delivers Around Its Arc

The spring pulls from the canopy along a fixed line, but only the part aimed along the bar's direction of travel turns the bar. Watch the spring force, the delivered tangential force, and its vertical share as the push-through bar sweeps all the way around its circle. Below the rest length the spring is slack and delivers nothing.

What you are seeing
  • The bar is a rigid arm hinged on the tower. Its end traces a full circle, and the spring runs from that end up to a fixed point on the canopy at (0, 63").
  • The delivered (tangential) force is the spring force projected onto the bar's direction of travel, so it is always less than or equal to the spring force. The rest is carried straight into the pivot and turns the bar no further.
  • The tangential vertical force is the vertical share of that delivered force, equal to the tangential force times cosφ. It is the part of the turning effort aimed up or down.
  • When the bar end comes within the spring's 25" rest length of the canopy, the spring is slack and the force is zero. At 90° straight up, with the pivot at 34" and a 24" arm, the end sits at 58", only 5" from the canopy, so the force reads 0.

These numbers illustrate how a spring's line of pull and the bar's geometry decide how much force reaches the bar and where it points. Real apparatus varies, and a full model would also account for the body's own leverage on the bar.
