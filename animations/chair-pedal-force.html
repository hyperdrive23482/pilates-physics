What you are seeing
  • The delivered (tangential) force is the spring force projected onto the pedal's line of travel, so it is always less than or equal to the spring force. The gap is force lost into the hinge.
  • The angle between the spring line and the tangent sets what fraction of the spring is delivered. The right-angle mark at the pedal shows the tangent is always perpendicular to the arm.
  • Vertical force equals the delivered force times cosφ. Two effects compete as the pedal rises: the spring line aligns better with the travel, so a larger fraction is delivered, but the spring also unstretches and cosφ shrinks. Where the vertical lift peaks depends on the spring and the geometry, and it is marked live on the graph.
  • The pedal cannot rise past the angle where the spring reaches its rest length, since a spring cannot be compressed. That top angle is set by the geometry, so it shifts when you move the anchor or change the rest length. It is marked "spring at rest" on both the graph and the mechanism.

These numbers illustrate how a spring's line of pull and the pedal's geometry decide how much support reaches the body and where it peaks. Real apparatus varies, and a full model would also account for the body's own leverage.
