Notes
  • Leg modeled as one straight rigid segment from hip to ankle. Thigh and total leg length are estimated from height using standard body-segment ratios (thigh is about 0.245 of height, total leg about 0.49 of height).
  • Leg weight is taken as 32% of body weight (both legs together). Center of mass sits 43% of the way down the leg from the hip.
  • Rope tension equals the full Balanced Body spring force at the computed stretch, using F = k·stretch + b per spring.
  • Spring stretch is the carriage's travel, not the foot's. The student rides the carriage, and the rope (foot over the pulley to the carriage) holds a fixed length, so lowering the legs drags the carriage toward the pulley. Because the hip travels with it, the spring sees much less motion than the foot does.
  • Schematic is a side view: a fixed pulley on the right, the sliding carriage with the hip on it to the left, and the springs anchored at the far left. CW torque rotates the leg toward flexion, CCW toward extension. Net is the imbalance the hip muscles must counteract.

For illustration of how torque at the hip responds to load and geometry. Humans vary, and more precise equations exist. Calibrated to a measured setup: pulley 10" above the carriage and 48" from the hip at 90 degrees, rope tie-on 24" from the hip, and about 17" of spring stretch at 90 degrees for a 5'9" student. Those are tunable constants in the script.
